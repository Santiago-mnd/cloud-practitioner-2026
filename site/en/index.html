One difference, one question

Which service records who took each action in your AWS account?

  • AAmazon CloudWatch
  • BAWS CloudTrail
  • CAWS Config

CloudWatch measures how resources behave; Config records their configuration. But the question asks “who did what”: CloudTrail records that.

Who did what? = CloudTrail.

three services, three different questions

The wrong answers sound plausible because they’re real services. That’s why each group of similar services has a phrase that sets them apart.

  • An SCP sets the permissions ceiling; it grants no permissions by itself.
  • AWS Budgets sends alerts; with actions, it can also stop instances.
  • Data transfer in from the internet to AWS is free; data transfer out costs money.
  • Shield Standard is included; Shield Advanced costs extra.

How to study with this guide

Follow these four steps. The third usually takes the most effort; it’s also the easiest to skip.

  1. 1

    Start with an analogy, not a fact

    If you can explain a concept using something from everyday life, you understand it. If you can’t yet, you may have only memorized the fact.

  2. 2

    Find the phrase that decides the answer

    Look for each topic’s short hint: “who did what?” separates CloudTrail from CloudWatch in two seconds.

  3. 3

    Write down every mistake, not every topic

    Your mistake log is worth more than any summary: it shows you exactly which questions could cost you points.

  4. 4

    Take a full mock exam without breaks

    Take the mock exam in one sitting, with a timer; you’ll see how you answer under exam time limits.

Many exams go wrong before the first question

You can also take the CLF‑C02 online with OnVUE, Pearson VUE’s software; your computer must meet its requirements. This list covers the rules Pearson VUE publishes for AWS and problems that come up in practice.

  • Run the system test on the same computer and network you’ll use for the exam.
  • No virtual machines. If you have WSL, Docker, or emulators installed, Hyper‑V may be active even if you never turned it on; disabling it requires a restart.
  • Avoid VPNs and corporate or public networks. Stop the VPN service: Tailscale and similar apps can restart even after you close their windows.
  • Start check‑in 30 minutes early. You’ll test your equipment, photograph yourself and your ID, and scan your room in 360 degrees.
  • Use one screen, no headphones, and a room to yourself. Bring an official ID with a name that exactly matches your booking.
