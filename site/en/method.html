How to study

On the CLF‑C02, you need to recognize cloud concepts, identify which AWS service fits a situation, and tell it apart from other plausible choices. The official guide leaves out programming, architecture design, and troubleshooting, so understanding a few ideas well will serve you better than reading the whole outline twice.

These four steps came from preparing for the AZ‑900, with an assessment at the start and another at the end. You can use them for this exam; pay particular attention to the third, since it takes the most effort and is easiest to skip.

  • 1. Start with an analogy

    If you can explain a concept through something familiar, you can recognize it when a question frames it differently. A memorized fact is much less reliable when you don’t understand it.

    Shared responsibility, explained with a kitchen: EC2 is like renting an empty kitchen; the cook maintains the stove, which stands for the operating system. With RDS, the kitchen comes equipped and AWS maintains it; you bring the recipe and ingredients—the data. With S3, you store your things in the pantry and decide who gets a key.

    So when a question asks, “Who patches the operating system?” think about who maintains the stove.

  • 2. Find the phrase that settles the choice

    For each topic, look for a hint that separates similar services. You have 65 questions in 90 minutes, about 83 seconds per question; a clear phrase helps you spot the answer without reasoning through every service from scratch.

    • Who did what? = CloudTrail.
    • Can be interrupted = Spot Instances.
    • Can wait hours = Glacier Flexible Retrieval or Deep Archive.

    You’ll find the notebook’s 45 hints in Differences, each with an explanation below it.

  • 3. Log every mistake, not every topic

    The official materials already summarize the exam topics. Your log has a different job: collect the questions you missed and reveal which choices you still confuse.

    After each mistake, write down your answer, the correct answer, and why your choice seemed reasonable. That last explanation helps you catch the confusion before it happens again.

    If you miss the same question twice and choose different answers, you’re probably guessing. Go back to the hint that separates those choices.

  • 4. Take a full practice exam without breaks

    Take the practice exam in one sitting, with a timer. That way, you can see how you respond under exam conditions, without breaks or looking things up.

    The passing score for the CLF‑C02 is 700 out of 1000: it’s a scaled score, not a percentage of correct answers. AWS doesn’t publish how many correct answers equal 700; for practice, aim to score above 80% on practice exams, and allow for a somewhat lower result on the real exam.

If the exam is a few days away

When the exam is close, decide where to spend your time. This order worked when preparing for the AZ‑900:

  • First, find out where you stand. Take an assessment to see which domain deserves your study time that afternoon, instead of dividing it equally between what you know and what you don’t.
  • Study one topic in short blocks, then answer questions. Instead of reading for thirty minutes straight, read for ten and answer five questions to check what you understood.
  • Skip new material the night before. It may shake your confidence, and there isn’t enough time for it to stick. Get some sleep that night.
  • On exam morning, use Hints only. You can read the hint view in Differences in about seven minutes.
